**Picked up the next-named milestone** (issue #25 Finding #4, explicitly queued by the prior two sessions): the planner front-loads elective hours ahead of remaining mandatory obligations — the prior session's own repro showed 203h against a 185h target, every semester pinned near the hard cap, and pinned the root cause precisely in `planner_goals.ts`'s `scorePlan`: goal 1 (`degree_completion`) is `Math.min(dh, degreeRequiredHours)` with no notion of *which* course produced the hours, and it ranks strictly above goal 2a (`requirements_mandatory`) in the lexicographic vector. During the greedy best-first search, a step comparing "add a 4h elective" vs. "add a 2h mandatory course" always preferred the elective (bigger g1 marginal), regardless of goal 2a, because g1 outranks it. The search could spend the *entire* degree-hours budget on electives before a single pending mandatory course was placed; by the time it had to place the deferred mandatory courses (forced once electives could contribute no more g1 credit), their hours landed on top of the already-filled target instead of being counted toward it — inflating the final plan.

**Root cause was real, but the prior session's own investigation had explicitly flagged the obvious mitigation ("cap goal 1's marginal near the target") as insufficient** ("a large elective can still outrank a small mandatory course early in the search, far from that boundary"). Verified this independently by hand-deriving the search step-by-step before writing any code — confirmed correct: a *static* near-the-final-target cap doesn't help early on. The fix implemented here is a *dynamic* reservation, not a static cap: `budget(state) = degreeRequiredHours - remainingMandatoryHours(state)`, recomputed from the state's own set of still-unplaced mandatory courses at every step (new `remainingMandatoryHours()` in `planner_goals.ts`, mirroring `placedHours`'s existing count_hours_once/root_course_id dedup). `g1 = Math.min(dh, budget)` instead of `Math.min(dh, degreeRequiredHours)`. Placing a mandatory course raises `dh` and `budget` by the same amount (full marginal credit, always); placing an elective only raises `dh` — once accumulated elective hours hit the shrinking budget ceiling, further electives stop earning ANY g1 credit, at which point only mandatory-course actions still advance the plan, forcing the search to interleave mandatory placement instead of deferring all of it to the end. At a fully-built (DONE) state every mandatory course is already placed, so `remainingMandatoryHours` is 0 and `budget` = `degreeRequiredHours` exactly — **byte-identical to the original formula for any complete/valid plan**, which is why this changes zero final-plan scores and only affects mid-search ranking among partial states with a pending mandatory course.

**Quantified before/after, not just reasoned about:** new `tests/api/planner_goals.test.ts` end-to-end test drives the real `PlannerWorker.run()` (lookahead disabled to isolate the exact scoring mechanism changed) against 3 mandatory 2h courses + 6 elective 4h courses, `degreeRequiredHours: 20`. RED-verified via `git stash` on just `api/ai/planner_goals.ts`: **old formula → 26h** (all 3 mandatory courses fully deferred, added on top of an already-electives-filled 20h target — a fully avoidable 6h overshoot). **New formula → 22h** (mandatory courses interleaved once the elective share is exhausted; only 2h left, the *unavoidable* atomicity slack of a single 4h elective landing right at the boundary — courses aren't divisible, so hitting the target exactly isn't always possible). A 66% reduction in avoidable overshoot in this scenario, and structurally the same mechanism scales to the real repro's much larger numbers (the fix bounds overshoot by one course's granularity, not by the sum of every deferred mandatory course's hours). A second, low-level unit test locks the `remainingMandatoryHours`/budget mechanism directly (documented as reinforcing, not by itself proving a behavior change — the old code already reached the right answer in that narrower comparison via the lower-priority g2a tiebreak; the PlannerWorker end-to-end test is the decisive evidence).

## 🔎 Finding #4 investigated, deliberately NOT implemented this session (2026-07-21)

Same session as the two entries below (Findings #1–#3 all fixed and merged: PR #27, #31, #32).

**Investigated issue #25 Finding #4** ("planner front-loads more elective hours than the degree needs" — 203h against a 185h target observed, every semester near the hard cap). Traced the root cause precisely in `planner_goals.ts`'s `GOAL_STACK`/`scorePlan()`: goal 1 (`degree_completion`) is `Math.min(degreeHours, model.degreeRequiredHours)` — correctly capped, no reward for overshooting the *final* target. But the vector is compared **lexicographically**, and goal 1 ranks strictly above goal 2a (`requirements_mandatory`) — so *during search*, while still under the cap, a marginal action that adds more raw hours (a large elective) always outranks one that advances a smaller pending mandatory course, regardless of goal 2a's difference, purely because goal 1's per-step marginal is compared first. This can let the search consume "hour budget" on electives before mandatory courses are placed, then need to open additional room (a new semester, or push a semester near the hard cap) to fit the mandatory courses that were deferred — inflating the total plan beyond the 185h target even though goal 1 itself never rewards the overshoot directly.

**Deliberately not implemented.** Unlike Findings #1–#3 (narrow, additive, single-function fixes), this is a change to the core planner's goal-priority/scoring semantics — reordering or reweighting `GOAL_STACK`/`scorePlan()` risks changing plan output broadly (semester balance, which electives get chosen, total hours) across every existing scenario, not just the over-hours case. This needs: (a) a decision on the *intended* tradeoff (e.g., should goal 2a's marginal contribution be considered before goal 1's raw-hours marginal, or should goal 1's marginal itself be capped per-step, not just in the final score?), (b) careful regression testing against every planner test suite that encodes current search behavior as ground truth (`planner_goals.test.ts`, `planner_scorecard.test.ts`, `generate_plan_load_distribution_policy.test.ts`, `generate_plan_dual_semester_load_balance.test.ts`, and likely more), and (c) before/after evidence across multiple realistic Hebrew scenarios, not just the one repro. A focus/avoid conflict on the same area (allowed by `AcademicInterestProfile`'s own contract — focus/avoid are independent lists) shows up fully in BOTH `matchedFocusAreas` and `avoidedAreas`, never hidden, even when they cancel out in the final clamped score.
